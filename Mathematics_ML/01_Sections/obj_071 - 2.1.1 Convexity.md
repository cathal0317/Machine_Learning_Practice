# 2.1.1 Convexity

## Conceptual overview
Convexity is a 'well-behavedness' property where any line segment between two points on the function graph lies above or on the graph. This subsection formalizes this property and demonstrates that it simplifies optimization by removing the distinction between local and global optima.

## Why it matters
Convex optimization is a mature field where we have strong guarantees about algorithm performance and optimality.

## Active recall
> [!question]- Why is convexity so desirable in machine learning?
> Because for convex functions, every local minimizer is also a global minimizer, meaning simple descent algorithms will not get stuck in suboptimal 'valleys'.

## Narrative flow
Definitions are followed by Theorem 13 (local is global). Then, differentiability is introduced to give practical calculus-based checks for convexity (Hessian positivity). Finally, optimality conditions for both unconstrained and constrained cases are derived.
