# 3.3.1 Three approaches to (numerical) differentiation

## Conceptual overview
Compares three methods for calculating gradients: finite differences (approximation), symbolic differentiation (manipulation of algebraic rules), and automatic differentiation (exact derivatives following code implementation).

## Why it matters
Understanding these differences is crucial for realizing why automatic differentiation is the engine behind modern deep learning frameworks.

## Active recall
> [!question]- What is the main drawback of symbolic differentiation?
> The resulting derivative expressions can become extremely large and memory-intensive to store and evaluate.

> [!question]- Why is finite differences often unsuitable for training large neural networks?
> It only provides an approximation of the gradient and requires $p+1$ evaluations of the function for $p$ parameters, which is computationally expensive.

## Narrative flow
Briefly describes the mechanics of finite differences, symbolic differentiation, and automatic differentiation, highlighting the advantages of the latter for computational efficiency.
