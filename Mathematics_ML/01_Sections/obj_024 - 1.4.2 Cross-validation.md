# 1.4.2 Cross-validation

## Conceptual overview
Cross-validation is a set of techniques for estimating the generalization risk when the underlying data distribution is unknown. It involves splitting the training data into 'folds', training the model on a subset, and validating it on the remainder to approximate the expected test error.

## Why it matters
It is the standard practical method for hyperparameter tuning and model selection, allowing practitioners to choose the 'sweet spot' in the bias-variance trade-off.

## Active recall
> [!question]- What is the natural global objective cross-validation attempts to optimize?
> It attempts to minimize the expected generalization error $\mathcal{R}(k) = \mathbb{E}_{D \sim P^n}(\mathbb{E}_{(X,Y) \sim P} (\ell(h_{k,D}(X), Y) \mid D))$.

> [!question]- Why is V-fold cross-validation preferred over hold-out validation?
> V-fold cross-validation reduces the variance of the error estimate by averaging over multiple splits, ensuring every data point is used for both training and validation.

## Narrative flow
The subsection defines the target objective for model selection and introduces hold-out validation. It then details the V-fold cross-validation algorithm and interprets its result as a Monte Carlo approximation of the expected generalization error.
