# 2.5 Stochastic gradient descent

## Conceptual overview
This section introduces SGD as a response to the computational inefficiency of full gradient descent on large datasets. It develops the algorithm, discusses practical considerations like stepsize schedules, and provides theoretical convergence guarantees in expectation.

## Why it matters
SGD is the fundamental optimization algorithm for neural network training and most large-scale machine learning tasks.

## Active recall
> [!question]- What is the 'Idea' behind SGD as presented in Subsection 2.5?
> The idea is to replace the expensive full gradient computation with a gradient of a single component function $\nabla f_{i_k}(w)$ where $i_k$ is sampled uniformly at random.

> [!question]- What property of the stochastic gradient ensures the algorithm can still converge to the correct minimum?
> The stochastic gradient is an unbiased estimator of the true gradient, meaning $\mathbb{E}(\nabla f_{i_k}(w)) = \nabla f(w)$.

## Narrative flow
The section starts with the practical motivation for SGD (efficiency), defines the algorithm, explains how to choose stepsize schedules, and then provides a formal convergence proof for strongly convex objectives. It ends with remarks on practical efficiency and non-strongly convex cases.
