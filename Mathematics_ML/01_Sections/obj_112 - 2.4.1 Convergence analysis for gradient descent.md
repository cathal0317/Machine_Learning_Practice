# 2.4.1 Convergence analysis for gradient descent

## Conceptual overview
This section analyzes the rate at which gradient descent iterates approach the optimal solution, specifically for functions that can be modeled as quadratic forms with symmetric positive definite matrices. It introduces the concept of linear convergence in the context of these well-structured problems.

## Why it matters
Understanding convergence rates is essential for predicting the computational cost of training and for understanding how the curvature of the objective function impacts optimization speed.

## Active recall
> [!question]- What is the specific form of the objective function analyzed in the quadratic convergence section?
> The objective function is assumed to be a quadratic form $f(w) = \frac{1}{2}\langle Cw, w \rangle - \langle w, b \rangle$, where $C$ is symmetric and positive definite.

> [!question]- What determines the convergence speed of gradient descent on a quadratic objective?
> The speed is determined by the eigenvalues of the Hessian matrix $C$, specifically the ratio between the minimum and maximum eigenvalues.

## Narrative flow
The section begins by restricting the analysis to quadratic objectives to simplify the derivation of convergence bounds. It then presents Proposition 20, which formalizes the linear convergence rate, and concludes by discussing how the matrix condition number affects this rate.
