# 2.2.1 Duality

## Conceptual overview
Duality allows an optimization problem to be viewed from two perspectives: the primal problem (minimization) and the dual problem (maximization). It introduces the Lagrange function to incorporate constraints into the objective via multipliers, leading to the dual function which always provides a lower bound to the primal optimal value. The section explores conditions under which these two perspectives reach the same optimal value, known as strong duality.

## Why it matters
Duality theory is fundamental for solving constrained optimization problems, often leading to more efficient algorithms and providing deep theoretical insights into the nature of constraints.

## Active recall
> [!question]- What is the difference between weak and strong duality?
> Weak duality is the property that the dual optimal value is always less than or equal to the primal optimal value, whereas strong duality is the condition where these two values are exactly equal.

> [!question]- What role does the Lagrange function play in duality?
> The Lagrange function combines the objective function and the constraints into a single expression using Lagrange multipliers, allowing the dual function to be defined as the infimum of this expression over the primal variables.

## Narrative flow
The subsection begins with the definition of the Lagrange function and multipliers. It then develops the dual problem and explores the relationship between primal and dual values (weak and strong duality). It concludes by introducing sufficient conditions for strong duality (Slater's condition) and necessary/sufficient conditions for optimality (KKT conditions).
