# 1.2.2 Characterization of the Bayes classifier

## Conceptual overview
This subsection provides the formal proof and interpretation of why thresholding the regression function $\eta(x)$ at 0.5 is optimal. It explores the implications of this optimality, including the 'Bayes risk' (the unavoidable error rate). It also includes a concrete example of calculating this classifier for a Gaussian mixture model, highlighting the practical difficulty of knowing the required probability densities.

## Why it matters
It formalizes the intuition that we should follow our 'best guess' probabilities to minimize errors.

## Active recall
> [!question]- What is the 'regression function' $\eta(x)$?
> It is the conditional probability $\mathbb{P}(Y = 1 \mid X = x)$.

## Narrative flow
Defines the regression function, proves its optimality via Proposition 1, notes the MAP connection, derives the Bayes risk formula, and works through a Gaussian mixture example.
