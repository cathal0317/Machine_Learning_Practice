# 1.3 Empirical risk minimization and hypothesis classes

## Conceptual overview
This subsection transitions from theoretical risk to practical computation. Since the true distribution $P_0$ is unknown, it defines the 'Empirical Risk' $\hat{R}(h)$ as the average loss over a finite training dataset. The 'Empirical Risk Minimizer' (ERM) $\hat{h}$ is then defined as the function within a chosen 'Hypothesis Class' $\mathcal{H}$ that minimizes this training error.

## Why it matters
ERM is the fundamental algorithm used to train nearly all modern machine learning models, from linear regression to deep neural networks.

## Active recall
> [!question]- Define the empirical risk $\hat{R}(h)$ for a dataset of size $n$.
> $\hat{R}(h) = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n \ell(h(X_i), Y_i)$.

> [!question]- What is an 'Empirical Risk Minimizer' (ERM)?
> The ERM is $\hat{h} \in \text{argmin}_{h \in \mathcal{H}} \hat{R}(h)$, the function in the class $\mathcal{H}$ that achieves the lowest training error.

## Narrative flow
Defines empirical risk, introduces the ERM principle, and discusses the necessity of choosing a hypothesis class $\mathcal{H}$ (like linear functions or K-nearest neighbors) to restrict the complexity of the learning problem.
