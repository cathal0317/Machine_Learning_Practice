# 3.1.2 Universal approximation

## Conceptual overview
This section introduces the Universal Approximation Theorem, which states that single-hidden-layer networks are dense in the space of continuous functions on compact sets. It covers historical proofs by Cybenko and Hornik and discusses the theorem's applicability to ReLU networks.

## Why it matters
It provides the theoretical guarantee that neural networks are capable of representing any reasonable continuous function given enough neurons.

## Active recall
> [!question]- Who are the key researchers credited with the early Universal Approximation results?
> Cybenko (using the Hahn-Banach theorem) and Hornik (using the Stone-Weierstrass theorem) in the late 1980s.

## Narrative flow
Introduces the concept of universal approximation, states the formal theorem for sigmoidal activations, and then extends the result to ReLU activations via a specific construction.
