# 1.4 Bias-variance trade-off

## Conceptual overview
The bias-variance trade-off is a decomposition of the expected test error into the noise of the data, the bias (error from model under-fitting), and the variance (error from model sensitivity to training data fluctuations). A more complex hypothesis class $\mathcal{H}$ generally reduces bias but increases variance.

## Why it matters
Understanding this trade-off is essential for model selection and regularization, as it explains why the most complex model is rarely the best choice for generalization.

## Active recall
> [!question]- What happens to variance and bias as the complexity of the hypothesis class $\mathcal{H}$ increases?
> As complexity increases, the model can fit the training data better (decreasing bias) but becomes more sensitive to specific training samples (increasing variance).

> [!question]- In the error decomposition, what does the 'noise' term represent?
> It represents the irreducible error $\mathbb{E}((h^*(X) - Y)^2)$ due to the inherent stochasticity of the target variable $Y$.

## Narrative flow
The section starts by motivating the trade-off through the K-NN example. It then formally decomposes the expected squared error into noise, bias, and variance components, followed by specific analyses of K-NN and cross-validation as tools for managing this trade-off.
