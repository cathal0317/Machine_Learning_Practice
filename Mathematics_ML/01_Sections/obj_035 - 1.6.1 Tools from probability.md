# 1.6.1 Tools from probability

## Conceptual overview
This subsection provides a technical toolkit of inequalities designed to bound the probability of large deviations for random variables. It focuses on non-asymptotic results that provide explicit constants for finite sample sizes, specifically introducing sub-Gaussian variables and Hoeffding's Inequality.

## Why it matters
These tools allow for rigorous performance guarantees on algorithms based on finite training data, moving beyond simple asymptotic intuition.

## Active recall
> [!question]- What is the difference between an asymptotic and a non-asymptotic result in this context?
> Asymptotic results (like CLT) describe behavior as $n \to \infty$, whereas non-asymptotic results (like Hoeffding's) provide explicit bounds that are valid for specific, finite $n$.

## Narrative flow
The subsection builds up from Markov's and Chernoff's bounds to define sub-Gaussian variables. It then introduces Hoeffding's Lemma for bounded variables and finishes with Hoeffding's Inequality for sums of independent variables.
